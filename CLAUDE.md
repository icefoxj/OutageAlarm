# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for Claude Code when working in this repository.

## The project

OutageAlarm is an audible power outage alarm. A USB charger in the wall outlet powers a TP4056 module, which keeps an 18650 cell charged, and energizes a relay. When power fails, the relay's NC contact closes and the buzzer sounds, powered by the battery. A TEST button simulates the outage.

Public repository: https://github.com/icefoxj/OutageAlarm (branch `main`).

## Language

**All project material is in English:** documentation, code comments, identifiers, file and folder names, schematic labels, commit messages, and the text engraved on the enclosure. The user may talk to you in Portuguese; answer them in their language, but keep everything committed to the repo in English.

## Current phase: v1 (simple)

- No microcontroller and no firmware:
  - detection: U1 IN+ → SW2 (TEST, NC pushbutton) → K1 coil → U1 IN−; LED1 + R1 straight across the 5 V, before SW2;
  - alarm: U1 OUT+ → SW1 (ARM) → K1 COM → K1 NC → BZ1 → U1 OUT−;
  - battery: BT1 18650 on U1 B+/B−.
- All of it on one perfboard (`hardware/electronics/perfboard.svg`) that covers the floor. The four shell screws pass through its corners, so the posts clamp it to the base.
- 3D-printed enclosure, modeled in OpenSCAD.
- There is no software yet. When there is (v2, ESP32), it goes in `firmware/`.

## Design principles

Do not break these without discussing with the user first:

1. **Fail-safe:** no 5 V = alarm sounding. Every change must preserve this.
2. **Battery always ready:** USB keeps the cell charged. On standby, nothing but U1's protection draws from the cell (µA).
3. **End-to-end test:** the TEST button must simulate a real outage (cut the coil), not just turn on the buzzer.
4. **No direct connection to 120/230 V mains.** Mains only enters through the certified USB charger. If a future version needs to sense mains directly, that requires isolation (optocoupler or transformer), a fuse and an explicit review. Point out the risks to the user.
5. **Li-ion cell only through the protection.** Loads connect to U1's OUT+/OUT−, never to B+/B−.
6. **Parts easy to source in Brazil** (local electronics stores, Mercado Livre, AliExpress).
7. **Simplicity before features.** New features go into v2 or v3, not v1.

## Conventions

- **Units:** mm, V, mA, Ω, with a decimal point (5.1 kΩ).
- **Designators:** U1, BT1, K1, D1, R1, LED1, BZ1, SW1 (ARM), SW2 (TEST). Use the same ones in the schematic, BOMs and wiring table.
- **Contacts:** NC (normally closed) and NO (normally open).
- **Commits:** imperative mood ("Add…", "Fix…").

## Keep everything in sync

When changing a part, a value or a connection, update all of these:

- `hardware/electronics/schematic.svg`
- the perfboard layout: the data in `perfboard.py` (then rerun it), the board table in `docs/electronics.md`, and `k1_hole`, `d1_hole`, `r1_hole` in `enclosure.scad`
- BOM and wiring table in `docs/electronics.md`
- BOM and behavior table in `README.md`
- if a dimension changes: parameters in `hardware/mechanical/enclosure.scad` and the tables in `docs/mechanical.md`
- if the livery changes: its description, part table and layer counts in `docs/mechanical.md`

## Mechanical (OpenSCAD)

- `enclosure.scad` is the source of truth. The STLs in `stl/`, the 3MFs in `3mf/` and the PNGs in `docs/img/` are generated from it and must not be edited by hand.
- Origin and axes are described in the file header: origin at the inner front-left corner, on the top face of the base (the shell's walls stand on it at z = 0).
- Parameters live in Customizer sections (`/* [Section] */`). Derived values live in `/* [Hidden] */`.
- Three printed parts, all modeled in their installed position:
  - the **top**: the control face, its chamfer, the buzzer ring and a lip;
  - the **sides**: the walls and the screw posts;
  - the **base**: dark, with a 45° undercut, and a pocket on top for the perfboard's solder side.
- The top and the sides are glued into the **shell** (user request: print the top separately to optimise the colour print). `shell(livery)` models the whole shell without the posts. `top()` and `sides()` split it along the crease under the face chamfer: the plane parallel to the face at `split_dz` = −`ce`.
- **Joint** (user request: a precise fit with room for glue):
  - A lip under the top fits inside the walls, `glue_gap` (0.15 mm) per side, with a lead-in chamfer.
  - The lip reaches up into the face. At the front, the inner chamfer covers only part of its thickness, and the rest would hang free when the top prints face down.
  - A groove in the top, over the inner edge of the walls, takes the excess glue.
  - The screw posts reach the seam and are notched for the lip (`post_gap`).
  - The inner chamfer's ridge under the seam belongs to neither part.
  - The `joint` view is a labelled section through the back wall (docs/img/joint.png).
- **Print orientation:**
  - `top_pose()`: the top prints face down.
  - `sides_pose()`: the sides print upside down on the seam (the same rotation, lowered by `edge_c`), so the seat is the first layer: flat and true.
  - Keep both orientations within 45° overhangs. Check the STLs: the earlier artifact of this kind was a strip on the lip root at the front.
- Shape:
  - The outer and inner surfaces are lofts (`loft()` through `rr()` outlines).
  - The front walls lie exactly on their 10° lean planes. On planes parallel to the face, `lean_z()` gives the height where they meet such a plane, and `in_ring()`, `slab()` and `slab_ring()` build exact rings and bands from it.
  - Approximating that height leaves slivers in the split.
  - Controls on the face are placed in top-view coordinates with `on_face(x, y)`. Marks on the front use `on_front(x, z)`, on the back `on_back(x, z)`.
- Every color goes through `paint()`, called with no transform around it, so the `cutaway` view can cut in assembly coordinates.
  - The top, the sides and the inlays use `paint(c, mesh = true)`, which renders them as meshes (CGAL): the preview (OpenCSG) can't resolve their hundreds of small strokes and paints the whole shell in the livery colour. The print parts use `render()` for the same reason.
  - Never put `render()` inside `paint()`: rendering a rendered part again fails in OpenSCAD 2021 ("The given mesh is not closed").
- **Mech livery (user request, like the [Tab5 + Stream Deck Neo stand](https://github.com/icefoxj/Tab5-Streamdeck-Neo-Stand)):** markings in the style of anime mechs (straight lines, 45° cuts, a title band with a unit code, a reticle, hazard stripes, chamfered lettering).
  - **Depth:** inlaid `inlay_depth` (0.6 mm, as requested) in a second filament.
  - **Where it's drawn:** `face_art()`, `front_art()` and `back_art()` draw it in 2D, in surface coordinates. The face uses (u, v): u = X and v = up the slope, with `fv(y)` converting a top-view y.
  - **Marks vs. livery:** the marks (labels, rings, wordmark, "5V") are in both versions. The decorative livery (band, dashes, hazard stripes, caption, the front slashes) is only in the two-colour one.
  - **Two versions:**
    - `top(false)` and `sides(false)` are one colour, with the marks engraved.
    - `top(true)` and `sides(true)` are recessed for everything.
    - `inlay(area)` is `shell_outer() ∩ art_recess(area)`, so each inlay fills its recess exactly. The face inlay goes with the top, the front and back inlays with the sides.
  - **Lettering:** the mech font is `mech_glyphs` (centre lines on a 6-unit grid, mitred strokes, clipped to the cell). It has only the glyphs used so far; add more as needed.
  - **Minimum sizes**, checked by asserts:
    - Face lettering: strokes and gaps of 0.8 mm. It prints on the bed, so it is whole in each of its 3 layers.
    - Wall lettering: 1.4 mm strokes and 0.7 mm gaps, because each layer cuts a wall inlay into a strip about 0.7 mm wide.
  - **Clearances:** livery layout and clearances are asserts too (gap `livery_gap` to edges, grille, labels, TEST ring).
  - **Keep the livery on the face.** Face graphics cost 3 layers in all. Every wall layer that holds an inlay costs up to two filament changes.
  - **Layer counts in the docs:** docs/mechanical.md quotes the two-colour layers per part:
    - top: 3, at Z 0.0–0.6 mm;
    - sides: 27 + 29 = 56, at Z 7.2–12.6 and 30.2–36.0 mm.
    Update them when they change: `make_3mf.py` prints them in CI.
  - **3MFs:** `make_3mf.py` writes `enclosure_top_2color.3mf` (`top`, `face_livery`) and `enclosure_sides_2color.3mf` (`sides`, `front_wordmark`, `back_5v`).
    - They are core 3MF (2015/02) with no slicer extensions: one named mesh per part, grouped in one object.
    - Creality Print, which the user runs with a CFS, loads that as one object with parts. Filaments are assigned in the slicer.
  - **Colours:** they are stored as base materials, as a hint only. Keep `WHITE` and `RED` in `make_3mf.py` in sync with `shell_color` and `livery_color`.
  - **Checks:** `make_3mf.py` fails if a mesh is open or flipped, if a printed part doesn't lie on the bed (z = 0), or if an inlay isn't exactly a 0.6 mm slab (it ran onto an edge, a chamfer or a hole).
- Generate the files locally (if OpenSCAD is installed):
  ```
  cd hardware/mechanical
  for p in top sides base; do
    openscad -o stl/enclosure_$p.stl -D "part=\"$p\"" enclosure.scad
  done
  for p in top_2color sides_2color inlay_face inlay_front inlay_back; do
    openscad -o parts/$p.stl -D "part=\"$p\"" enclosure.scad
  done
  python3 make_3mf.py parts 3mf
  ```
- Without local OpenSCAD (the case on the user's machine): push, then download the CI artifact with `gh run download <id> --name enclosure-render --dir <folder>`.
  - Copy `stl/*.stl` to `hardware/mechanical/stl/`, `3mf/*.3mf` to `hardware/mechanical/3mf/` and `img/*.png` to `docs/img/`.
  - Check the PNGs before committing. The artifact also has the intermediate `parts/*.stl`; don't commit them.
  - The artifact also repeats any STL or 3MF already in the repo, so delete the ones a change made obsolete.
  - CGAL doesn't write facets in a stable order. If an STL's facets are the same as the committed one, keep the committed file.
  - `gh run list` can still show the previous run for a few seconds after a push: match the run to the commit (`headSha`).
- CI runs with `--hardwarnings`, and also fails on any `ERROR` or `WARNING` line: OpenSCAD 2021 can exit 0 after either. A warning that stops a PNG render leaves an empty image, so check the PNGs.
- Critical clearances are `assert()`s in `enclosure.scad`. A layout change that breaks one fails the render. Add an assert for every new clearance.
  - Near the top: buzzer vs. holder and board, relay vs. buzzer, front wall and D1, ARM switch vs. TP4056, TEST button vs. the board and its parts, holder and TP4056 vs. screw posts and each other, screw heads vs. the undercut.
  - Also near the top, the perfboard: inside the walls, its cut corners vs. the shell's rounded corners, board around the screw holes, the base's rim under its outer row, screw length vs. the posts.
  - Also near the top: the top's lip vs. the relay, the holder, the buzzer and the ARM switch, and the buzzer ring staying on the top.
  - In the livery layout section: the livery.

## Releases

- Tags are `vMAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` on `main`, annotated. The project phase (v1, v2) is the major version.
- Before tagging:
  - check that CI passed on `main` and that the committed STLs, 3MFs and PNGs come from that run;
  - move the `[Unreleased]` notes in `CHANGELOG.md` to the new version, with the date, and update the compare links at the bottom.
- Create the GitHub release from the tag with `gh release create`.
  - Attach the five print files: `stl/enclosure_{top,sides,base}.stl` and `3mf/enclosure_{top,sides}_2color.3mf`.
  - The release notes are for makers: what's in it, which files to print, what isn't tested yet.

## Schematic

- Hand-drawn SVG on a 10 px grid. Classes: `u` = 5 V USB wires (orange), `b` = battery wires (blue), `w` = symbol strokes, `wf` = filled symbols.
- To check the drawing: `msedge --headless=new --user-data-dir=<temp folder> --screenshot=out.png --window-size=1100,730 file:///<path>/schematic.svg` (the PNG may take a few seconds to appear).
- When the circuit grows (v2), move to KiCad in `hardware/electronics/kicad/`.

## Perfboard

- `perfboard.py` holds the layout as data, in holes (i, j) from the board's front-left corner: i to the right, j to the back. Run `python3 perfboard.py` to redraw `perfboard.svg`. Don't edit the SVG by hand.
- The SVG is 1:1 in mm on A4 landscape, so a print at 100 % is a cutting and drilling template. It has two views: the component side, and the solder side mirrored. Links are orange (5 V side) or blue (battery side), like the schematic.
- The board is 45 × 26 hole spacings, centered in the shell. The posts stand over the holes 2 in from each corner, and the corners are cut at 45° through the second hole.
- The SRD relay's pins are off the 2.54 mm grid: the drawing and the model place them from its footprint, centered on 5 × 5 spacings.
- Check the drawing with msedge as for the schematic (`--window-size=1123,794 --force-device-scale-factor=3` gives a sharp A4).
