# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for Claude Code when working in this repository.

## The project

OutageAlarm is an audible power outage alarm. A USB charger in the wall outlet powers a TP4056 module, which keeps an 18650 cell charged, and energizes a relay. When power fails, the relay's NC contact closes and the buzzer sounds, powered by the battery. A TEST button simulates the outage.

Public repository: https://github.com/icefoxj/OutageAlarm (branch `main`).

## Language

**All project material is in English:** documentation, code comments, identifiers, file and folder names, schematic labels, commit messages, and the text engraved on the enclosure. The user may talk to you in Portuguese; answer them in their language, but keep everything committed to the repo in English.

## Current phase: v1 (simple)

- No microcontroller and no firmware:
  - detection: U1 IN+ → SW2 (TEST, NC pushbutton) → K1 coil → U1 IN−; LED1 + R1 straight across the 5 V, before SW2;
  - alarm: U1 OUT+ → SW1 (ARM) → K1 COM → K1 NC → BZ1 → U1 OUT−;
  - battery: BT1 18650 on U1 B+/B−.
- All of it on one board that covers the floor: the PCB (`hardware/electronics/pcb/`, KiCad 8, Gerbers in `fabrication/`) or the perfboard (`hardware/electronics/perfboard.svg`), with the same outline, holes and part positions. The four shell screws pass through its corners, so the posts clamp it to the base.
- 3D-printed enclosure, modeled in OpenSCAD.
- There is no software yet. When there is (v2, ESP32), it goes in `firmware/`.

## Design principles

Do not break these without discussing with the user first:

1. **Fail-safe:** no 5 V = alarm sounding. Every change must preserve this.
2. **Battery always ready:** USB keeps the cell charged. On standby, nothing but U1's protection draws from the cell (µA).
3. **End-to-end test:** the TEST button must simulate a real outage (cut the coil), not just turn on the buzzer.
4. **No direct connection to 120/230 V mains.** Mains only enters through the certified USB charger. If a future version needs to sense mains directly, that requires isolation (optocoupler or transformer), a fuse and an explicit review. Point out the risks to the user.
5. **Li-ion cell only through the protection.** Loads connect to U1's OUT+/OUT−, never to B+/B−.
6. **Parts easy to source in Brazil** (local electronics stores, Mercado Livre, AliExpress).
7. **Simplicity before features.** New features go into v2 or v3, not v1.

## Conventions

- **Units:** mm, V, mA, Ω, with a decimal point (5.1 kΩ).
- **Designators:** U1, BT1, K1, D1, R1, LED1, BZ1, SW1 (ARM), SW2 (TEST). Use the same ones in the schematic, BOMs and wiring table.
- **Contacts:** NC (normally closed) and NO (normally open).
- **Commits:** imperative mood ("Add…", "Fix…").

## Mechanical (OpenSCAD)

- `enclosure.scad` is the source of truth. The STLs in `stl/`, the 3MF in `3mf/` and the PNGs in `docs/img/` are generated from it and must not be edited by hand.
- Origin and axes are described in the file header: origin at the inner front-left corner, on the top face of the base (the shell's walls stand on it at z = 0).
- Parameters live in Customizer sections (`/* [Section] */`). Derived values live in `/* [Hidden] */`.
- **House look (user request 2026-11-24: "seguir o visual dos outros projetos"):** like the other kits (`E:\GitHub\CLAUDE.md`, "Mech look"): a graphite body with its corners cut at 45° in top view (`corner_c`), **grey** armor plates with cut corners (grey, never white: user rule 2026-11-24, in `E:\GitHub\CLAUDE.md`), and the livery in house yellow #FFCC00 (never orange) inlaid only in the plates.
- Five printed parts, all modeled in their installed position:
  - the **shell** (`shell()`): the control face, its chamfer, the buzzer ring, the walls and the screw posts, in one piece (graphite);
  - the **base**: a darker graphite, with a 45° undercut, and a pocket on top for the perfboard's solder side;
  - three flat **armor plates** (grey + yellow): on the face, the front and the back. Each is modeled flat in its own frame (visible face at z = `plate_t`, two pegs underneath), placed with `place_plate()` and printed with `plate_pose()`. The pegs go into blind holes in the shell (`peg_holes()`).
- Outlines are octagons: `co()` cuts the corners of a rectangle, `oc()` gives the cut after an inset, `cut_rect()` cuts each corner by its own amount (the plates). `poly_inset()` and `poly_margin()` inset convex polygons and measure points inside them.
- **One-piece shell** (user decision 2026-11-24, "pode juntar o topo e a lateral"): from v1.0.0 to the house look the shell was split into a top and sides glued with a lip, to keep the colour print short. With the colour on the armor plates, the split went away. Don't split it again without asking.
  - `shell()` = `shell_outer()` minus the cavity (the buzzer ring stays), plus `screw_posts()`, minus the holes, the plates' peg holes and the insert holes.
  - The posts stand on the perfboard and reach up into the face (`screw_posts()` is clipped by `shell_outer()`).
  - **M3 heat-set inserts** (user request 2026-11-24, "insert m3 para fechar a tampa"): each post takes an M3 × 5.7 × 4.6 mm brass insert in its bottom (`insert_d` 4.0 mm hole, `insert_l` + 0.5 mm deep, flush with the post's end), then a `screw_clear` hole for the tip of the M3 × 16 countersunk machine screw. `post_d` = 7 gives 1.5 mm of wall around the insert (an assert); it is limited by the 18650 holder and the TP4056 module at the back.
- **Print orientation:** `shell_pose()`: the shell prints face down. The face is the first layer, the face chamfer is 45° and the walls lean at most 30°; the posts grow from the face. Keep it within 45° overhangs, and check the STLs after a change.
- Shape:
  - The outer and inner surfaces are lofts (`loft()` through `co()` outlines).
  - The front walls lie exactly on their 10° lean planes. On planes parallel to the face, `lean_z()` gives the height where they meet such a plane, and `in_ring()` builds exact rings from it (the cavity's inner chamfer).
  - `crease_dz` is the crease under the face chamfer.
  - Controls on the face are placed in top-view coordinates with `on_face(x, y)`. The front plate goes on with `on_front(x, z)`, the back plate with `on_back(x, z)`.
  - `face_chamfer()` projects the outline where the walls cross the seam plane onto the face and insets it by `edge_c`, so every edge of the face chamfer, the corner cuts too, is exactly 45° to the face.
  - The face plate holds the rocker: the face is cut away under it, so `plate_t` must not exceed `rocker_panel_t`. TEST goes through the face and the plate (4.4 mm).
- Every color goes through `paint()`, called with no transform around it, so the `cutaway` view can cut in assembly coordinates.
  - The shell, the plates and the inlays use `paint(c, mesh = true)`, which renders them as meshes (CGAL): the preview (OpenCSG) can't resolve the hundreds of small strokes and paints whole parts in the livery colour. The print parts use `render()` for the same reason.
  - Never put `render()` inside `paint()`: rendering a rendered part again fails in OpenSCAD 2021 ("The given mesh is not closed").
- **Mech livery (user request, like the [Tab5 + Stream Deck Neo stand](https://github.com/icefoxj/Tab5-Streamdeck-Neo-Stand)):** markings in the style of anime mechs (straight lines, 45° cuts, a title band with a unit code, a reticle, hazard stripes, chamfered lettering).
  - **Depth:** inlaid `inlay_depth` (0.6 mm, as requested) in a second filament.
  - **Where it's drawn:** `face_art()`, `front_art()` and `back_art()` draw it in 2D, in plate coordinates. The face plate uses face coordinates (u, v): u = X and v = up the slope, with `fv(y)` converting a top-view y. The front and back plates are drawn around their centers.
  - **Marks vs. livery:** the marks (labels, rings, wordmark, bolt and "5V") are in both versions of the plates. The decorative livery (bands, dashes, hazard stripes, caption, brackets, chevrons) is only in the two-colour one. The shell carries no marks.
  - **Two versions:**
    - `plate(area, false)` is one colour, with the marks engraved.
    - `plate(area, true)` is recessed for everything.
    - `plate_inlay(area)` is `plate_blank(area) ∩ plate_recess(area, true)`, so each inlay fills its recess exactly.
  - **Lettering:** the mech font is `mech_glyphs` (centre lines on a 6-unit grid, mitred strokes, clipped to the cell): the same glyphs as the other kits' `cad/mech.py` (A–Z, 0–9, `- / . : < > +`).
  - **Minimum sizes**, checked by asserts: strokes of 0.8 mm at least. The plates print face down, so each stroke is whole in each of the inlay's 3 layers.
  - **Clearances:** livery layout and clearances are asserts too (gap `livery_gap` to the plates' edges and corners, the grille window, labels, TEST ring, brackets).
  - **Keep the livery on flat plates printed face down.** All of it then costs 3 layers. Don't put inlays on the walls again: every wall layer that holds one costs up to two filament changes.
  - **Layer counts in the docs:** docs/mechanical.md quotes the two-colour layers: 3, at Z 0.0–0.6 mm, for all three plates in one print. Update them when they change: `make_3mf.py` prints them in CI.
  - **3MF:** `make_3mf.py` writes `enclosure_plates_2color.3mf`: three objects (face, front and back plate), each made of its plate and its livery, laid out side by side on the bed.
    - It is core 3MF (2015/02) with no slicer extensions: one named mesh per part, grouped in one object per plate.
    - Creality Print, which the user runs with a CFS, loads each plate as one object with parts. Filaments are assigned in the slicer.
  - **Colours:** they are stored as base materials, as a hint only. Keep `GREY` and `YELLOW` in `make_3mf.py` in sync with `plate_color` and `livery_color`.
  - **Checks:** `make_3mf.py` fails if a mesh is open or flipped, if a plate doesn't lie on the bed (z = 0), or if an inlay isn't exactly a 0.6 mm slab (it ran onto an edge, a chamfer or a hole).
- Generate the files locally (if OpenSCAD is installed):
  ```
  cd hardware/mechanical
  for p in shell base plate_face plate_front plate_back; do
    openscad -o stl/enclosure_$p.stl -D "part=\"$p\"" enclosure.scad
  done
  for p in plate_face_2color plate_front_2color plate_back_2color inlay_face inlay_front inlay_back; do
    openscad -o parts/$p.stl -D "part=\"$p\"" enclosure.scad
  done
  python3 make_3mf.py parts 3mf
  ```
- Without local OpenSCAD (the case on the user's machine): push, then download the CI artifact with `gh run download <id> --name enclosure-render --dir <folder>`.
  - Copy `stl/*.stl` to `hardware/mechanical/stl/`, `3mf/*.3mf` to `hardware/mechanical/3mf/` and `img/*.png` to `docs/img/`.
  - Check the PNGs before committing. The artifact also has the intermediate `parts/*.stl`; don't commit them.
  - The artifact also repeats any STL or 3MF already in the repo, so delete the ones a change made obsolete.
  - CGAL doesn't write facets in a stable order. If an STL's facets are the same as the committed one, keep the committed file.
  - `gh run list` can still show the previous run for a few seconds after a push: match the run to the commit (`headSha`).
- CI runs with `--hardwarnings`, and also fails on any `ERROR` or `WARNING` line: OpenSCAD 2021 can exit 0 after either. A warning that stops a PNG render leaves an empty image, so check the PNGs.
- Critical clearances are `assert()`s in `enclosure.scad`. A layout change that breaks one fails the render. Add an assert for every new clearance.
  - Near the top: buzzer vs. holder and board, relay vs. buzzer, front wall and D1, ARM switch vs. TP4056, TEST button vs. the board and its parts, holder and TP4056 vs. screw posts and each other, screw heads vs. the undercut.
  - Also near the top, the perfboard: inside the walls, its cut corners vs. the shell's cut corners, board around the screw holes, the base's rim under its outer row, screw length vs. the inserts, wall around the inserts.
  - In the livery layout section: the plates on their surfaces (the face plate inside the flat part of the face, the front and back plates clear of the corners), the plate thickness vs. the rocker's clips, and the livery.

## Perfboard

- `perfboard.py` holds the layout as data, in holes (i, j) from the board's front-left corner: i to the right, j to the back. Run `python3 perfboard.py` to redraw `perfboard.svg`. Don't edit the SVG by hand.
- The SVG is 1:1 in mm on A4 landscape, so a print at 100 % is a cutting and drilling template. It has two views: the component side, and the solder side mirrored. Links are orange (5 V side) or blue (battery side), like the schematic.
- The board is 45 × 26 hole spacings, centered in the shell. The posts stand over the holes 2 in from each corner, and the corners are cut at 45° through the second hole.
- The SRD relay's pins are off the 2.54 mm grid: the drawing and the model place them from its footprint, centered on 5 × 5 spacings.
- Check the drawing with msedge as for the schematic (`--window-size=1123,794 --force-device-scale-factor=3` gives a sharp A4).
